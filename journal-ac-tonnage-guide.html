Buying guide

How to choose the right AC tonnage for your room

28 Oct 20266 min readBy the ACE engineering team

The most common mistake we see is not a bad brand or a bad installation. It is the wrong size. An undersized AC runs flat out all afternoon and still feels weak. An oversized one cools the air quickly but switches off before it has removed the humidity, so the room feels cold and sticky.

What "ton" actually means

In air conditioning, one ton of refrigeration (1 TR) is the cooling needed to melt one ton of ice in 24 hours. In engineering units that is 12,000 BTU per hour, or about 3.517 kW of heat removed. A 1.5 ton AC can remove roughly 18,000 BTU of heat every hour.

The quick rule of thumb

For an average room in a hot, humid town like Kumbakonam, a common starting point is:

Room areaStarting point
Up to ~100 sq.ft0.75 – 1 ton
100 – 150 sq.ft1 ton
150 – 200 sq.ft1.5 ton
200 – 300 sq.ft2 ton
Above 300 sq.ftMultiple units, cassette or ductable

Treat this as a first guess, not the answer. Five things push the number up or down.

The five factors that change the size

  1. Sun exposure. A west-facing wall or large glass window can add a lot of heat in the afternoon. Shaded rooms need less.
  2. Top floor. A concrete roof that bakes all day radiates heat into the room well into the night. Top-floor rooms usually need a step up.
  3. People. Each person adds body heat. A bedroom for two is very different from a clinic waiting room for fifteen.
  4. Appliances and lighting. Kitchens, computers, display lights in showrooms and TVs all add load.
  5. Ceiling height and insulation. High ceilings mean more air volume; poorly sealed doors let cool air escape.
Try it yourself: our AC size calculator applies these factors to give you an indicative tonnage in seconds.

Why bigger is not better

An oversized AC "short-cycles": it hits the set temperature fast and stops, then restarts a few minutes later. Every restart costs energy, and because the coil is not cold for long enough, it removes less moisture. In the Delta's humidity, that means a room that feels damp even at 24 °C. Inverter ACs reduce this problem by slowing down instead of stopping, but correct sizing still matters.

When you need a heat-load survey

For anything larger than a single room, such as a marriage hall, showroom, hospital ward or office floor, rules of thumb break down. Our engineers do a proper heat-load calculation that accounts for occupancy peaks, kitchen and lighting loads, fresh-air needs and running hours. That is how we size systems from a few tons to 140 TR and above.
